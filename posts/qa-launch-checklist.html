The QA Checklist I Run Before Every Launch

Sep 2026·6 min read

Launch day has a way of exposing everything you hoped nobody would notice. The contact form that silently fails on Safari. The checkout button that sits half off screen on a small phone. The confirmation email that never sends. Every one of these was catchable, and every one of them costs you trust the moment a real user finds it first.

Over time I have distilled my pre-launch routine into a single checklist. It takes about half a day to run properly, and it has never let me down.

The Core Checklist

  • Critical paths first: sign up, log in, purchase, contact. Each one tested start to finish with real data.
  • Every form: required fields, invalid input, successful submit, and the confirmation message or email that follows.
  • Cross-browser pass: Chrome, Safari, Firefox, and Edge at minimum, with real devices for iOS and Android.
  • Responsive sweep at 320px, 768px, 1024px, and 1440px. Check tap targets, truncated text, and horizontal scroll.
  • Error states: kill your internet connection mid-submit and see what the user sees.
  • Performance: run Lighthouse, fix anything red, and compress every image over 200 KB.
  • Analytics and tracking: confirm events actually fire by watching them land in the dashboard.

How I Prioritize What I Find

Not every bug blocks a launch. I sort findings into three buckets: blockers that break a critical path, majors that degrade the experience, and minors that can ship with a ticket attached. This keeps the team calm and the launch date honest, because a typo on the About page should never delay a release while a broken payment flow absolutely should.

The 24 Hour Rule

After the checklist passes, I let the product sit for a day and then do one final pass with fresh eyes, preferably on a different device. It is remarkable what you notice when you are no longer staring at the same screen. That final pass has caught some of the most embarrassing bugs of my career, quietly, before anyone else saw them.

QA is not about perfection. It is about making sure the first impression your product makes is the one you intended.
