Responsive Design Is Not a Feature, It Is the Foundation

Jul 2026·5 min read

More than half of your visitors are holding a phone. Yet so many sites are still designed on a big monitor and squeezed down at the end, with tiny tap targets, text that wraps awkwardly, and that dreaded sideways scroll. Users can feel when mobile was an afterthought, and they leave.

Mobile First in Practice

  • Design the smallest screen first. If it works at 360px, scaling up is easy. The reverse is painful.
  • Tap targets at least 44px, with breathing room between them. Thumbs are not cursors.
  • Type that scales with the viewport using clamp, so headings feel intentional on every device.
  • Images that are cropped and compressed for mobile, not just the desktop file squeezed smaller.
  • One column is not a compromise. On a phone it is clarity.

Testing Beyond Resizing Your Browser

Dragging a desktop browser window narrower is a start, but it misses the real stuff: touch scrolling, the on-screen keyboard covering your form, and Safari doing something unexpected. Test on at least one real iPhone and one real Android before launch. Five minutes on actual hardware reveals what hours of simulation cannot.

Responsive design is not extra work. Done right from the start, it is simply how the work is done.
