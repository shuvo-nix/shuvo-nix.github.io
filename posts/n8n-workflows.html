5 n8n Workflows That Save Me 10 Hours a Week

Sep 2026·5 min read

Automation has a branding problem. People imagine complex robots replacing entire jobs, when in reality the best automations are boring little pipes that move information from one app to another so you never have to copy and paste again. Here are the five n8n workflows that quietly run my week.

The Five Workflows

  • Lead intake: a new contact form submission creates a Notion record, sends a personalized acknowledgment email, and pings me on Slack with the details. Response time went from hours to seconds.
  • Content repurposing: when a blog post goes live, the workflow drafts three social snippets from the summary and drops them into a planning sheet for review.
  • Social scheduling: approved snippets get queued across platforms on a schedule, with a reminder if the queue runs empty.
  • Invoice nudges: unpaid invoices get a polite follow up after seven days, automatically, so I never have to write that awkward email again.
  • Weekly digest: every Monday at 8am a summary of site traffic, form submissions, and open tasks lands in my inbox.

Start Embarrassingly Small

The mistake most people make is automating a giant process first. Start with the smallest, most annoying task in your week, the one that takes five minutes but happens twenty times. Automate that. Once you feel the relief, the next workflow sells itself.

Ten hours a week sounds like a bold claim until you add up the minutes. Automation does not give you superpowers. It gives you back your attention.
