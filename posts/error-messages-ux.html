Error Messages That Don't Make Users Panic

Jun 2026·4 min read

Something went wrong might be the laziest sentence in all of software. The user hit a wall, and the product responded with a shrug. The worst part is that the user assumes it is their fault, or worse, that your product simply cannot be trusted with their data.

The Formula

Every good error message answers three questions, in order, in plain language:

  • What happened: We could not save your changes. Not a code, not a stack trace, a sentence.
  • Why it happened, if known: Your connection dropped, or that file is over the 10 MB limit. Context removes fear.
  • What to do next: Try again, check your connection, or contact support with this reference. Never leave a user at a dead end.

Tone Under Pressure

Errors are the worst moment to be clever. Skip the jokes when someone might have lost work. Be calm, be specific, and take responsibility where you can: Our server hiccuped lands very differently from Invalid request. One builds trust in a bad moment, the other spends it.
