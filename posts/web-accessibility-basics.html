Web Accessibility Basics Every Site Should Pass

Jul 2026·6 min read

Accessibility often gets treated as a compliance chore, something to deal with later. But about one in six people live with a disability, and far more benefit from accessible design every day, anyone in bright sunlight, on a slow connection, or holding a baby in one arm. Accessible sites are simply better sites.

The Five Minute Audit

  • Contrast: body text should hit a 4.5:1 ratio against its background. Light gray on white fails almost every time.
  • Alt text: every informative image needs a description. Decorative images get an empty alt so screen readers skip them.
  • Focus states: press Tab through your site. If you cannot see where you are, neither can a keyboard user.
  • Labels: every form field needs a real label, not just placeholder text that vanishes when you type.
  • Keyboard navigation: menus, modals, and buttons must all work without a mouse.
  • Heading order: H1, then H2, then H3, no skipping. Headings are how screen reader users scan your page.

Accessibility Is Good Business

Beyond being the right thing to do, accessibility overlaps heavily with SEO, usability, and conversion. Clear structure helps search engines. Visible focus helps everyone filling a form. Readable contrast helps every user outside on a phone. When you fix accessibility, you rarely fix it for just one group.

Run the audit this week. Most of what you find takes minutes to fix and matters for years.
